## Cross-checks

**SpaceX share count, fourth confirmation.** At 9/30, with the exact captured AUM
($15,618,951,279 → 56.1792M shares out): 36,938,300 × 150.86 / (1.096 × 278.02) =
**32.55%** of gross against **32.6%** disclosed. Unchanged at 3/31, 6/30, 8/31 and 9/30.

**10/9 shares-out had to be derived.** The Morningstar scraper was down 10/08–10/16 (their
markup changed, see below), so there is no captured AUM for 10/9. With the filed count, the
disclosed 35.9%-of-net SpaceX line implies 55.8597M shares out (±0.045M from rounding),
i.e. ~$16.73B AUM. Against the 10/7 capture (56.0105M) that is ~−$45M over two sessions.

**Did the fund trade between 9/30 and 10/9?** Converting each disclosure to absolute share
counts and testing the gap against the combined rounding error (each weight is ±0.05pp):
**1 of 24 names exceeds 2σ (ACGL, z = −2.25) — ~1.1 expected by chance.** TSLA agrees to
+0.1%, SPOT to 0.0%, HEI-A to 0.0%. No evidence of trading. Buy-and-hold holds again.

That is what makes the combination legitimate: the two disclosures are **two independently
rounded measurements of the same book.**

## The 9/22 distribution — the finding that almost misled the backtest

The first backtest showed every basket jump ~+1.8/BPTIX on **9/22** and stay there — v5 by
+1.70, v6.0 by +1.89 — regardless of holdings. Cause: **BPTIX went ex-distribution on
2026-09-22, $1.888/share** (Yahoo events; the prior one was $4.169 on 2025-12-16). Yahoo's
`close` is the unadjusted NAV, so it drops by the payout; static per-BPTIX baskets don't.
Shares-out confirms it: 56.0551M (9/21) → 56.5121M (9/23), the reinvestment issuing new
shares.

Correction, exact when the payout is reinvested: holdings and borrow per BPTIX are diluted by
the same factor, **Yahoo's adjclose ratio 0.993391**. A basket anchored before the ex-date is
multiplied by it on and after the ex-date; one anchored after is divided by it before.

**Uncorrected, v5 looked ~1.8/BPTIX wrong for all of late September and October. Corrected,
its error over that stretch is −0.05 to −0.34.** It never drifted that badly; the NAV did.

## Backtest (distribution-adjusted), every session 8/31 → 10/19

RMS level error, $/BPTIX:

| window | n | v5 | v6.0 | v6.1 | v6.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| early Sep (v5's home) | 11 | **0.150** | 0.337 | 0.388 | 0.385 |
| late Sep | 10 | 0.155 | **0.110** | 0.143 | 0.134 |
| 9/30–10/9 (v6 anchors) | 8 | 0.217 | **0.024** | 0.058 | 0.052 |
| **10/12–10/19 pure out-of-sample** | 6 | 0.148 | 0.143 | **0.075** | 0.081 |

The nested pattern a periodic re-anchor should produce, at both levels:

- v5 is best on its own early-September turf; v6 takes over from late September.
- After 10/9, the **10/9-anchored** baskets beat the **9/30-anchored** one by ~2×.

## Choice: v6.2

v6.1 and v6.2 are indistinguishable out of sample (0.075 vs 0.081, n=6; the gap is well
inside the RMS standard error). So the choice falls to construction, and the stated goal is
the lowest-variance unbiased estimator: **v6.2 uses both disclosures**, the z-test supports
pooling them, and pooling cuts each weight's rounding variance roughly in half.

## Watch

every session. Six days is not enough to call it, and the same pattern in v5 in mid-September
