How to read it

X = ARKK spot, Y = delta-neutral P&L per share. Each option is hedged with stock at its trade delta, so at the current spot & base borrow every line passes through ~$0. The curvature is gamma (long options = smile-up: you make money on a big move either way); the vertical shift when you move the borrow slider is the forward/skew effect.

Borrow is the edge here. ARKK borrow is ~2% today. If it spikes (hard-to-borrow into a squeeze), the forward drops and puts re-rate up, calls down — at 300% borrow the 83 put gains ~$9 at unchanged spot while the 83 call loses ~$1.3. So if you think borrow goes UP, owning puts (or put spreads) is the cleaner expression; if borrow stays put, it's a pure gamma/vol view.

How bad can ARKK borrow actually get? — and why the ETF wrapper caps it

Realistically ARKK borrow stays low — low-single-digit % — almost all the time. It's a large, liquid ETF on hundreds of millions of shares; even when Michael Burry put on a well-known ARKK short in 2021 it remained borrowable (a "general collateral"–type name, not a special). So the base case is the ~2% on the screen, and a "stress" is more like 5–15%, not 300%.

Why an ETF's borrow is structurally capped — the create/redeem arbitrage. Unlike a single stock with a fixed float, an ETF's share count is elastic. Authorized Participants (APs) can create new ARKK shares any day by delivering the underlying basket (Tesla, etc.) to ARK in kind, or redeem shares back into the basket. If shorting demand makes ARKK hard to borrow, the borrow fee rises → it becomes profitable for an AP/lender to create new shares and lend them out (capturing the fee) → supply of lendable shares goes up → the fee falls back. That feedback loop is the safety valve a single stock doesn't have, which is why ETF borrow rarely runs away.

So when would borrow genuinely spike? Only if the arbitrage breaks: the underlying basket itself becomes hard to source or halts, creations get suspended, or a violent crowded short overwhelms create capacity short-term. For ARKK (liquid US large/mid-cap basket) that's unlikely — the high end of the toggle (50–300%) is a deliberate stress test / what-if, not a forecast. It's there to show the shape of the borrow→forward→put/call effect, and would be far more realistic for a hard-to-borrow single name (or for SpaceX itself right after IPO).

Mechanism summary: ETF borrow fee is bounded by creation economics; single-stock borrow is bounded only by float. The toggle spans 0–300% so you can price both regimes.
