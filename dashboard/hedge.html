How to read it

Why this trade. BPTIX's NAV = public holdings + SpaceX + leverage. By shorting the public holdings in their fund weights, you cancel the public-market P&L and are left long the part you can't buy directly: SpaceX (and the other privates) + the fund's leverage/fee drag. The net long notional (long − short) is roughly that un-hedgeable slice.

What the Total line means. If it drifts up, the private book (mostly SpaceX, still marked at the stale $1.25T) is being marked up or the fund is outperforming its public hedge; if it drifts down, the hedge is over/under-sized or fees/leverage are bleeding. A big jump would come when BPTIX re-marks SpaceX to the $1.77T IPO price after 6/12.

Hedge drift — your fixed short vs your per-share public exposure

Your short is a fixed share count from 5/20, and you hold a fixed 130k BPTIX shares. As the fund takes inflows it issues new shares to new investors and buys public stock with their cash — that does not add public exposure to your shares. Your per-share public exposure rises only because SpaceX (fixed at $3.89B) is diluted across more shares. So the public book embedded in your position, per share, is what your fixed short must match — and it has drifted by the red bars below.
