Implied perfect-hedge leverage — is the hedge ratio actually dynamic?

Instead of assuming a fixed leverage (1.1335) and calling the short "under-hedged", flip the question: what leverage would make the fixed short an exact hedge each day? Solve L* = SpaceX wt + short notional ÷ (130k × BPTIX NAV) daily. Read it against the assumed 1.1335 (dashed) and 1.00 (unlevered, dotted): implied < assumed → the fund carries more public beta than the short covers → under-hedged (long dominates); implied > assumed → over-hedged (short dominates).

What it shows: the implied leverage is a smooth ~1.0 (slightly declining), not a swinging line — i.e. the short was sized to roughly the fund's unlevered (net) public book, so it structurally under-hedges the levered (1.1335×) fund, a little more each day as the book grows. So the day-to-day dominance flips you see in the P&L are NOT a leverage signal — strip the SpaceX re-mark (toggle above) and the residual wobble is basket tracking error + the SpaceX/leverage carry, not the leverage ratio swinging. How to read it

Why this trade. BPTIX's NAV = public holdings + SpaceX + leverage. By shorting the public holdings in their fund weights, you cancel the public-market P&L and are left long the part you can't buy directly: SpaceX (and the other privates) + the fund's leverage/fee drag. The net long notional (long − short) is roughly that un-hedgeable slice.

What the Total line means. If it drifts up, the private book (mostly SpaceX, still marked at the stale $1.25T) is being marked up or the fund is outperforming its public hedge; if it drifts down, the hedge is over/under-sized or fees/leverage are bleeding. A big jump would come when BPTIX re-marks SpaceX to the $1.77T IPO price after 6/12.

Caveats: excludes borrow/financing on the shorts, dividends, and BPTIX's own leverage & fees; share counts are the combined 2-account totals; prices are Yahoo daily closes.
